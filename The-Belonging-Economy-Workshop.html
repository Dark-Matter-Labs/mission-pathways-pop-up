Workshop proposal · Dark Matter Labs for South Yorkshire Narrative & Place Brand

The Belonging
Economy

A day for Dark Matter Labs and South Yorkshire to test how connected people feel to the place they live in — and how that sits alongside how the world perceives it from outside.

Day · 10:00–16:00 3 DM co-hosts on site Barnsley · Doncaster · Rotherham · Sheffield Prep + day + follow-up: £3,500
01 · The starting problem

Every major place-reputation index tells you the same story: the value of a place's economy, measured through its reputation. They're built for nations and for cities the world already knows how to notice — and none of them are place-led: the measures and the data points belong to the index publisher, not to the place being measured.

Sheffield, as SY's core city, already carries most of the region's external reputation — and is investing to move it. Marketing Sheffield is putting money behind tracking how its Sheffield Inspires campaign shifts outside perception, with Chief Exec backing, because what lifts one of our places should lift all of them. That's real progress, but it's an external, campaign-facing measure. It says nothing about how connected people feel to the place they actually live in.

This workshop starts from the belief that both are measurable — and that a region's economy of connectedness, resident-facing and borough by borough, can be built alongside that external work, not instead of it.

02 · What the day is built to answer
1

What is the economy of a place, once wellbeing, agency and connectedness sit next to GDP and GVA?

Define the measure before we chase it — drawing on existing global reputation-value studies, but built for a region of four boroughs rather than one global city.

2

What portfolio of work is intrinsic to South Yorkshire, and needed to build the future its people actually want?

From high-value jobs to a trained, possibly employed, cadre of community connectors — the roles and interventions that move the sense of place, not just the score.

3

Could a place-led perception measure potentially combine with a resident-facing index, rather than running as two separate reports?

Not a rebrand exercise — a test of whether a bespoke panel, an adapted version of an existing methodology, or something else entirely can track Sheffield's outward campaigns and the region's resident indices without inventing two scorecards that never speak to each other.
